---
title: The Middle Ages and the Castrum Sancti Viti (6th-9th century)
description: From Saracen raids to the birth of the fortified village dedicated to Saint Vitus.
data: 0850-01-01
---

In the 6th century, during Lombard rule, the area was marked by an administrative
control system that has left traces still visible today in local place names,
such as the area known as "La Corte", which recalls the Lombard term *curtis*.

In the 9th century, Saracen raids, which reached from the coast into inland Lazio
as far as the Subiaco area, pushed the inhabitants to take refuge higher up, where
they first sheltered in the natural caves of the hill. There they then rebuilt the
settlement around a fortress perched on the top of the cliff, with the help of
Benedictine monks. It was the Benedictines
who dedicated the new village to Saint Vitus, giving rise to the name **Castrum
Sancti Viti**, the "Castle of Saint Vitus", from which the town's name derives.
The village remained a Benedictine fiefdom until 1180.

---
title: The churches of San Vito Romano (15th-18th century)
description: From the Church of San Biagio to the Sanctuary of Compigliano, the religious buildings that mark the life of the village.
data: 1609-01-01
---

The Church of **Santa Maria de Arce**, the oldest in the village, dates back to
around 1400: originally built with a single nave to serve the castle garrison, it
is linked to the Theodoli family's noble residence, whose main entrance looks out
onto the church. It was restored between 1861 and 1903, when the side aisles were
added, and again in 1916 by the architect Carlo Busiri Vici; it houses a notable
painting of the Assumption attributed to Carlo Maratta or his school.

The Church of **San Biagio**, protector against ailments of the throat, was
rebuilt between 1607 and 1609 on the remains of a thirteenth-century oratory; its
bell tower, dating from 1715, holds three bells, the oldest from the early 18th
century. Every year, on 3 February, the traditional blessing of the throat is
still held there.

On top of the hill stands instead the Church of **San Vito**, patron saint of the
village, built between 1725 and 1735 in place of an older chapel and celebrated on
15 June. Inside, the octagonal ceiling with the "Glory of the Saint" stands out;
the church has no bell tower, and its bells are housed in a pyramid-shaped raised
section of the roof.

The renewal programme promoted by Cardinal Mario Theodoli also included the
construction of the Church of **Santi Sebastiano e Rocco**, octagonal in plan with
Baroque decoration and celebrated on 16 August, together with the adjoining
Carmelite convent: after the annexation to the Kingdom of Italy, this became the
seat of the town hall in 1872.

Finally, on the Colle di Compigliano stands the **Sanctuary of the Madonna di
Compigliano**, begun in the 16th century and completed in the early 1600s.
Tradition holds that the Virgin appeared among the branches of a cherry tree to a
mute shepherd boy, giving him back his voice; the sacred image, painted on a
cherry-wood panel, was crowned by the Vatican Chapter in 1948 and is still a place
of devotion for the villagers today.

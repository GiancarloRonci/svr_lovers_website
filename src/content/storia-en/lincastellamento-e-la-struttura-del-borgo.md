---
title: The hilltop settlement and the shape of the village
description: A village that grew across several hills, between the medieval core beneath the castle and the 17th-century quarter of Mario Theodoli.
data: 1649-01-01
---

San Vito Romano developed according to the model of hilltop fortified settlement,
spreading across several hills: Monte San Vito, the Colle di Compigliano, the
Torricella and Le Cese. The oldest core, still called "nnabballe" ("down below")
by the villagers, lies beneath the castle, among outcropping sandstone and
picturesque loggias that preserve the appearance of the medieval village.

The seventeenth-century quarter, known instead as "nnammonte" ("up above"), was
born with the opening of Borgo Mario Theodoli and stretches towards the two
branches of the provincial Empolitana road, showing how Cardinal Mario Theodoli's
urban project expanded the village beyond the limits imposed by the original
rocky outcrop. An inscription still visible in Piazza Augusto Baccelli recalls the
cardinal's work with these words: "Cardinal Mario Theodoli levelled the harshness
of the mountains, opened the roads, and having levelled the hills turned them into
houses, and there raised a temple to the divine dispeller of the plague. In the
year of the Lord 1649."

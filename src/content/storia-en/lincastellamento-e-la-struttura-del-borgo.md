---
title: The hilltop settlement and the shape of the village (17th century)
description: A village that grew across several hills, between the medieval core beneath the castle and the 17th-century quarter of Mario Theodoli.
data: 1649-01-01
---

San Vito Romano developed around the fortified core of the castle, gradually spreading over the surrounding heights, including Monte San Vito, the Colle di Compigliano, the Torricella and Le Cese.

The oldest core, still called “nnabballe” (“down below”) by the inhabitants, lies at the foot of the castle and preserves, among sandstone outcrops and characteristic loggias, many elements of the old medieval village. This core, first laid out under the Colonna, runs from the church of San Biagio and is closed by the Porta della Mola and the Porta Olevano.

From the 17th century, the town expanded beyond the limits of its original core. The new quarter, called “nnammonte” (“up above”) by the inhabitants, developed above all with the building of the Borgo Mario, promoted by Cardinal Mario Theodoli. From 1640 the cardinal had a straight axis opened, starting from the gate of the medieval core at the foot of the castle: from there the 17th-century quarter climbs until it splits into the two branches of the Empolitana provincial road, one towards Genazzano and the other towards Pisoniano. The new urban axis made it possible to extend the town beyond the cliff on which the medieval village had been concentrated, and profoundly altered the structure of the settlement.

The cardinal died in 1650, before the works were finished. They were carried on by his nephew Carlo Theodoli, who inherited his estate, together with Carlo's son Girolamo, an architect. The quarter took its name from the cardinal.

An inscription still visible in Piazza Augusto Baccelli, dated 1649, celebrates the work of Cardinal Mario Theodoli with these words:

“Cardinal Mario Theodoli levelled the harshness of the mountains, opened the roads, and having levelled the hills turned them into houses, and there raised a temple to the divine dispeller of the plague. In the year of the Lord 1649.”

The inscription is a particularly significant record of the great urban transformation that San Vito Romano underwent during the 17th century: from the compactness of the medieval village gathered around the castle to the gradual expansion of the town towards the higher areas.

*Source for the gates of the medieval core, the extent of the 17th-century quarter and the works after 1650: the “Il Centro Storico” information panel of the Municipality of San Vito Romano (network of the Borghi dei Monti Prenestini e della Valle del Giovenzano).*

---
title: From San Vito to San Vito Romano
description: The Unification of Italy, the new name of 1872, and the 20th century between civic memory and war.
data: 1872-05-16
---

After the end of papal administration and annexation to the Kingdom of Italy in
1871, the town took on its current name, **San Vito Romano**, by Royal Decree
of 16 May 1872, to distinguish it from the many other Italian municipalities
that shared the name San Vito.

Among the most illustrious figures linked to the town is **Guido Baccelli**,
Minister of Public Education, who in 1898 established Italy's first "National
Tree Festival", celebrated for the first time on 21 November of that year.

The 20th century also brought painful pages: during the Second World War the
town suffered reprisals by German troops, with civilian victims on 1 May and 5
June 1944, episodes that remain part of the community's collective memory to
this day.

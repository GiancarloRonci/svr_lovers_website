## Meaning of the Poem

“A Teca ella Madonna e gliu’ Rosariu” (The Madonna's Shrine and the Rosary) tells of a tradition of devotion handed down in San Vito for generations: the “teca” of the Madonna, a small wooden shrine with an image of the Virgin, which goes from house to house for the recitation of the rosary. Each family keeps it for three days, then hands it on to the neighbours and the round begins again.

The author remembers when it came to his home. His mother had already got ready to welcome it; as soon as she received it she set it on the little table, filled a glass with oil and lit the wick, the little lamp that stayed lit in front of the image. The shrine had small doors decorated with ribbons, and when his mother opened them, stars shone around the Madonna. All that was missing was the start of the rosary: his father was already sitting by the fireplace, his mother made the sign of the cross, and meanwhile another village woman came in through the front door to pray with them.

The shrine knew every house in San Vito, and the villagers had a deep devotion to that “wandering shrine”: when their turn came they waited for it at the door, rosary beads already in hand, with the glass ready in a corner, full of oil and with the wick standing upright. The devotion began first thing in the morning, when the woman of the house got up and was already in the kitchen with a candle stub ready, and the Madonna arrived with her stars around her head.

In San Vito, faith in the Madonna and the rosary is still alive: the shrine goes down to the Attaceca, climbs back up to the top at [Santa Maria](/en/luoghi/santa-maria-de-arce/), crosses the whole old village on the Monte of San Vito and comes back down below [San Biagio](/en/luoghi/piazza-san-biagio/). But by now the round is over: missing are all the people who have bolted their front doors and closed their shutters too. In those houses not a breath can be heard; the candle stub has burned down and the wick has gone out. The poem ends with this image of the extinguished lamp, in nostalgia for a village that has emptied.

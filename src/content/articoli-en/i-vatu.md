## The text in Sanvitese

<figure class="didascalia">
<div class="poesia-originale i-vatu"><div class="foglio">
<p class="titolo">I VATU</p>
<pre>NAVOTA SOTTO A GLIU’ VATU ERA TUTTU UN FRATTONE, CIABITEA I RAGHENU E GLIU' LIPERONE. I
SURICI STENO ANNASCUSI DENTRO PELLE MACERE, ELLE LINCERTE STENO HA PIGLIA’ I SOLE SOPRE
ALLE PIETRE. MARRECORDO UN NABBALLERO CI’ FICEA PURE CHE' PIANTINARU, MENTRE
SARREGGEA NFACCIA ALLA MACERA SENNO' GLI CASCHEA I CAPU. NAVOTA SOTTO A GLIU’ VATU I
NABBALLERI CI' GLETTENO DE' TUTTU, I STIVALETTI RUTTI E CHE’ OSSO E PRECIUTTU. CHE' RINALE
SBUCIATU SARREMEDIEA, COMME CISTEA BENE SOPRE A CHELLA TERRA. ANDO’ CI’ NASCEA DE’
TUTTU, MARRECORDO E MARASCHE ERENO DUCI COMME LO’ ZUCCHERU. NA’ PIANTA E PERSICA
CHELLE DURACI, ERA PIU’ BELLA DE’ MILLE VASI. A GLIU’ VATU ERENO BEGLI PURE I SPINI, L’ ESTATE
PORTENO NGROPPA CERTI BEGLI ROMICHI. ERENO ROSSI COMME GLI’ RECCHINI ELLE
CAPRANICOTTE, ECCO APPRECHE’ SE VEDENO PURE DE’ NOTTE. SO’ PROPRIO SICURU, NAVOTA A
GLIU’ VATU CISTENO TANTE PIANTE E SAMMUCU. ANDO’ SE’ POGGENO I CELLITTI A BECCANE,
MENTRE UN MEROLO SE’ METTEA A FISCHIANE. COMME ERA BEGLIO NAVOTA I VATU, CO’ TUTTA
CHELLA VERDURA PIANTATA SEMBREA UN QUADRU. COMME ERA BRAVU CHIGLIU PITTORE,
SCEGNEA SOTTO A GLIU’ VATU COGLIU ZAPPONE. I PASSONE LA’ SERA GLIU’ LASSEA NFILATU ALLA
MACERA, SENNO’ A MATINA APPRESSO GNARETROVEA. CHESTA STORIA E’ TUTTA PAESANA, CHI’
PIANTEA I PUMMIDORI CHI’ METTEA A NZALATA. I FASORI A SORICCHIU GLI’ PIANTEA I PITTORE,
GLI’ BATTEA E MANI PURE NOSTRO SIGNORE. SPISSU SAGGREGHEA PURE SAN BIAGIO, MO’ LASSO
PERDE SENNO’ PIGLO E PIAGNO.</pre>

## Explanation in English

The poem “I Vatu” takes us on a journey through the countryside of the old days, among uncultivated plots, stones, thistles, brambles, wild flowers and wild fruit. A landscape that looks untidy, yet is full of life, colour and wonder, where every plant and every animal found its own place. In the background the village of San Vito Romano rises above the hills, while a farmer works the land and tends the crops, deeply bound to nature and to the rhythm of the seasons.

From the blossoms of spring to the green of summer, from the warm colours of autumn to the silence of winter, the memory of a simple, genuine world resurfaces, made of work, traditions and knowledge handed down from generation to generation. It is a fond recollection of a countryside that was not just land to be farmed, but part of the identity of a community, of its roots and of its history. A beauty to be rediscovered, cherished and passed on, just as this poem in the San Vito dialect teaches us.

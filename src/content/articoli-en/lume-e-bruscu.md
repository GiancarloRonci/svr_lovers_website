## Meaning of the Poem

The poem “Lume e Bruscu” takes its title from that uncertain hour before dawn when it is no longer night and not yet day. At that hour a village woman is already up: she lights the fire in the hearth, bakes the cornmeal pizza and also puts a herring on the embers. Her husband has already led the donkey out of the stable and she is ready with her basket. So begins the “parade” the villagers made every day along the stairway, on their way to the fields.

Below Santu Martinu you can just begin to make something out, but it is still half an hour down to Cheifuci. Not a breath of air stirs on the road, and all you can hear is a sparrow whistling to its sweetheart and a blackbird already awake; it is so dark you can hardly see where you are putting your head. Meanwhile the villagers already have the day's work on their minds, from the manure for the seedbeds to the seeds to fetch.

The poem then follows the people of San Vito across the countryside around the village: another line is already down at Leprata, those heading for Pentima are still below the Salara, the carter with his mule is in the middle of the village, others have reached the Cunetta di Sant'Antonio, are below Viadoro or down at the Serva Rossa, and for Fagnanu there is still a long way to go. Setting out in the dark was a ritual: people got up before the cock crowed, slept with one eye open and kept their tools behind the door, ready for the next morning.

With affection and a smile, the author recalls that all this now lies behind us, and that the parade lasted a long time. In the end he stops, because telling it all would risk driving him mad.

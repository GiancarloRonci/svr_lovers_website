## Meaning of the Poem

“I Pressepio” is the nativity scene that [Don Marcello](/en/articoli/don-marcello-schiavella/) set up every Christmas in the church of [San Biagio](/en/luoghi/piazza-san-biagio/). The author remembers it with wonder: Don Marcello kept inventing new scenes and worked on it all year round, and people stood spellbound in front of that miniature San Vito.

The whole village was in the nativity scene. There was the stream of Surzu and, further down, the waterfall; the Via Romana crossing the bridge, with villagers and mules climbing back up towards the village; the women going to the fountain, one with a basket on her head and the other with a bag on her back. Below Porta Olevano a villager knelt at the foot of the cross with a candle, and its glow lit up the fountain and Palemacchia, where three or four women were at work. And then the alleys and the “arringhi”, which nobody could ever forget, with a swarm of children and girls skipping rope; a little further on, one woman seemed to be saying “see you soon”, while another slipped into the Ghetto.

Thinking back on it brings tears to the author's eyes. The crowd was so large that people could not even fit into Piazza San Biagio: some went in and out of the church to see the nativity scene, others waited on the steps of the Stretta, a little further back. That nativity scene, he says, was never really taken apart: even if we no longer see it, it is still there, all lit up, in the memory of those who were there.

The poem ends with a memory that brings a smile. Among all those moving figurines, one year Don Marcello even managed to make the Baby Jesus cry: the Madonna kept rocking him in vain, because the cord of the mechanism had broken, and the Baby was left crying for who knows how long.

The site on which the castle stands was probably occupied as early as the high Middle Ages: a document from 1085, preserved in the Regesto Sublacense, records the donation of some lands to the Monastery of Subiaco, evidence of the control exercised by the Benedictine monks over the "castrum Sancti Viti". Towards the end of the 12th century the fief passed to the powerful Colonna family, who, considering the existing structures insufficient, strengthened the defences with new perimeter walls. According to some historians and local tradition, Pope Martin V was born within these very walls, although the claim remains a matter of historical debate.

In 1563 the Colonna sold San Vito to the Massimo family, who ten years later, in 1573, passed it on to the Theodoli marquises, still the owners of the castle today. Cardinal Mario Theodoli promoted the construction of "Borgo Mario", built on an artificial terrace beside the fortress, while Carlo Theodoli gave the building its present appearance: the distinctive ship-like shape with its "prow" jutting towards the main street, the loggia, the exedra-shaped access ramps and the Guelph battlements crowning the top.

The interiors, closed to the public as they remain the private residence of the Theodoli family, house frescoes and decorations partly attributed to the painter Crescenzio Onofri. The arched loggias look out over Corso Mario Theodoli, while the main entrance, at the back of the building, faces the Church of Santa Maria de Arce. With its imposing mass and scenic position, the castle remains the most recognisable image of San Vito Romano, visible from the outside from many points around the village.

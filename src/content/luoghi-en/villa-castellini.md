Villa Castellini is one of the historic and prestigious manor houses of San Vito Romano. It is part of the town's rich heritage of villas, linked to the noble and distinguished families, such as the Baccelli, who chose this area as a summer retreat.

Along the road that runs through the town, a crenellated tower with a clock, bells and a rooster weathervane announces the villa, which stands out for its distinctive Neo-Gothic style. The building, painted in warm pink tones, features arched windows with chequered decorations, colourful shutters and a small brick wing topped by a bell tower with a cross.

Beyond the courtyard, overlooked by an arched portico, lies a charming park adorned with statues and fountains, descending in terraces along the slope. Tree-lined avenues shaded by lime trees, cypresses, cedars and pines alternate with stone stairways, box hedges and brick pillars topped with flower pots. In the middle of a clearing stands a fountain with a round basin covered in water lilies and a sculpture of dolphins holding up a bowl, while on a terrace potted roses frame a row of ancient columns. From the more open spots in the park, the view reaches the historic centre and Theodoli Castle.

As a private property, the villa is not always open to the public. In September 2025 it exceptionally opened its doors to host “100 anni di Rintocchi” (100 Years of Chimes), a cultural and charity event organised by the Casa Arcadia APS association that brought together art, history, theatre and inclusion. On that occasion, residents and visitors were able to admire the splendid condition of the house, beautifully renovated and enhanced. Locals also remember the villa as a much-loved setting for wedding photos.

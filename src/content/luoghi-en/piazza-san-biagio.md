Piazza San Biagio is one of the hearts of San Vito Romano's old town: a small square paved in cobblestones, overlooked by the church of San Biagio Vescovo e Martire, built on the site of a 12th-century oratory and rebuilt between 1607 and 1609 to a design by the architect Giovanni Theodoli. The square itself was redeveloped in 1646, and the church was formally consecrated on 13 October 1777.

The Baroque interior houses an altarpiece depicting Saint Blaise with a woman and a child, recalling the miracle of the healing of the throat associated with the saint's cult: every year, on 3 February, the tradition of the blessing of the throats is renewed. The side chapels of the Rosary and of Saint Joseph were added in 1831 and 1847 respectively, while between 1927 and 1929 the interior was enriched with marble altars and new flooring.

By day the square is a quiet crossing point among the alleys of the old town; in the evening, with the streetlamps lighting up the stone façades, it becomes one of San Vito Romano's most evocative corners, especially during festivals, when it fills with colourful bunting.

The church of San Vito is one of the landmarks of San Vito Romano and, together with its surroundings, one of the village's hearts.

## History

The present church was built in 1735, replacing an earlier building, to a design by Marquis Gerolamo Theodoli (1677-1766), a well-known architect. It stands on the top of the hill overlooking the village, and its structure is still the original one.

The façade is simple, marked out by side pilasters and a pediment decorated with a shell motif.

Inside, some eighteenth-century decoration survives: the late Baroque octagonal canvas in the centre of the ceiling, with the Glory of Saint Vitus, the decoration of the altar and the chapel of Saint Lawrence. The rest of the church is adorned with nineteenth-century decorations and works, while the seven oil-on-canvas works by Aronne Del Vecchio date from 1945.

At the centre of the altar stands the bronze statue of the patron saint, made in 1947 by Giuseppe Pirrone to replace the original statue in papier-mâché and fabric, which was lost in a fire in 1943.

This information comes from the information sign along the [Up the Monte to the Church of San Vito](/en/percorsi/al-monte-chiesa-san-vito) walk, which leads from the village centre up to the church.

## Surroundings

The lanes around the church, paved with cobblestones and lined with iron railings, offer evocative views over the terracotta rooftops and the surrounding hills, with Castello Theodoli standing out in the distance.

Il Bar 2000 is one of those places that live mostly in the memory of the people who spent time there: a vintage postcard shows it with its red sign, the covered terrace with its outdoor tables, and the small square it overlooked on Via dell'Arringo, halfway between Piazza Roma and "la Terrazza", next to the petrol station - right where the bank stands today - back when San Vito Romano moved at a different pace. For generations of sanvitesi it was simply "il bar 2000" — or, as some remember it, "the legendary bar 2000".

Those who remember it describe a welcoming place, cared for down to the smallest detail, inside and out: the tables stood on a floor of white gravel, in the shade of the trees, while a small side door opened discreetly onto the bar's wall, framed by climbing ivy that covered the walls in different colours with each season. Sitting there with the family for an ice cream, taking in the beauty of the surrounding landscape along with the sweetness of the ice cream, felt like stepping into a painting.

Memories shared by the community suggest the bar was already a well-loved meeting point from the early 1970s: between 1972 and 1978, during the summers when San Vito Romano was full of young people, everyone moved between Bar 2000 and the Terrazza da Faroni. A group of around thirty people, including the town's theatre group, used to gather here regularly, welcomed by Vincenzo, his wife, and Battista. Downstairs there was a room with a jukebox and a coin-operated payphone booth, where whole generations spent entire afternoons playing table tennis, cards, and billiards, dropping coins into the jukebox to dance close to the records, amid platonic crushes and dreamy afternoons.

For many it was also the place for first dates: some say they met their partner right there, others came all the way from Bellegra or Rome to meet friends and cousins, others came to visit relatives spending their holidays in San Vito, and some recall countless carefree evenings together — sometimes after borrowing a beach umbrella for a day at the seaside.

Winter had its own charm too: every weekend, along with hot chocolate with whipped cream for the children and a glass of red wine with roasted chestnuts for the grown-ups, a red candle stayed lit on the tables, and some even remember a snowfall that caught everyone there. The bar closed sometime between the late 1990s and 2000 — some remember it still open in December 1996, others recall 1998, and others place its closing right in 2000 — but for those who lived it, it remains a timeless place.

When this photo was shared in the community's Facebook group, many people found a piece of their own youth in it: some called it "the best years of our lives", others hold memories tied to summers back in town, and some simply wrote "time passes, things change, good memories remain". Some see in that transformation the start of a wider decline for the town, and some, looking at these photos, wonder what those walls could tell if they could speak. For anyone visiting San Vito Romano, it was still an obligatory stop: a small but meaningful symbol of the San Vito Romano of yesterday.

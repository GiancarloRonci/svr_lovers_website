*"A Scifella stea abballe agliu Canale."* The Scifella, a black creature with wings, lived down by the Canale, where the village women went to wash their clothes. And the women were afraid whenever they went there: they always carried a stick hidden in the *bagnarola*, the washtub, and slipped a few stones to throw into the pocket of their *parnanza*, their apron, because while they were washing, *"veneno assopre pure e garafole"* rose up from the water.

## The Scifella's tricks

The story of the Scifella is still frightening. Many a time the women found their sheets all twisted up inside the washtub, and their *corroglia*, the cloth ring used to carry loads on the head, undone *"comme na' fiétta e agli"*.

The Scifella was *'mpunita*, mischievous, and played tricks on everyone. The women who went to fetch water at the *cannelle*, the little fountains, knew it well: she would smash their water jugs and never let a single one off. And if she noticed they were scared, she would even steal *o cetratu* from them: *"'nne ficea sarva una"*, she spared no one.

## The night of the watermelons

Once the villagers brought *cucummeri*, watermelons, down to the Canale. One evening the Scifella wanted a slice, but the villagers were waiting for her with torches and sticks: *"piglià na scallata e botte ché ancora nsarefredda"*, she got scorched and took such a beating that she still hasn't cooled down.
